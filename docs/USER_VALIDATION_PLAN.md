# ExceptionOS — user validation and launch plan

Prepared October 21, 2026. These are proposed milestones, not completed outreach or measured traction.

## Audience and useful outcome

Start with applicants waiting on a hiring or education decision. The useful result is knowing what is blocking the process and taking the next appropriate action. A recorded reply should change the next-action plan when it changes the situation.

The first pilot should recruit 10–15 people who have an actual stalled application and are willing to try Anna. Avoid collecting application documents or personal correspondence centrally; ask testers to enter only the details they choose in their private workspace.

## Pilot protocol

Give each tester the app link after approval. Ask them to:

1. Describe their desired result, current situation, and previous attempt.
2. Build a plan and say whether the first action is useful and possible.
3. Paste a reply they choose to share with Anna. Compare its interpretation and source quotes with the original.
4. Draft and edit a response. Ask whether they would use it, what they changed, and how long it took. Do not require sending a message as part of the test.
5. Choose a follow-up date and find the goal in Attention today. Rebuild a plan if the reply changes it.
6. Close and reopen the app and verify their history.
7. Return after 3–7 days and report whether they took a real-world next step.

Ask, without leading them:

- What were you waiting on before opening the app?
- Which action, if any, was useful enough to do?
- Did the rebuilt plan reflect your update or repeat something already known?
- Did the interpretation reflect what the original reply actually said?
- Would you use this response? What did you have to change before it was useful?
- Could you tell what required action today and what was still waiting?
- At any point, were you unsure whether something had saved?
- What would make you choose this over a notes app or an Anna chat?

Record only pseudonymous tester codes, consented feedback, observed failures, and aggregate counts. Do not copy private goals into a shared research sheet.

## Proposed acceptance thresholds

| Measure | Pilot target | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Finish creation and planning unaided | At least 8 of the first 10 testers | Observed session or tester confirmation |
| Say the next action is concrete and useful | At least 7 of 10 | Answer plus a short reason |
| Updated plan reflects new context | At least 8 of 10 rebuilds | Tester confirms before/after difference |
| Response draft is useful after editing | At least 7 of 10 | Tester judgment plus edits needed; record time to a usable draft |
| Understand the blocker and follow-up state | At least 8 of 10 | Tester explains both in their own words |
| History returns after reopening | Every tested session | Reopen check; any failure blocks release |
| Take a real-world next action within a week | At least 5 of 10 | Voluntary follow-up |

These are internal product goals, not Anna's reward criteria. No user results have been measured in this work.

## Challenge requirements and calendar

The current Founding Builder program is based on approval and qualified usage rather than a ranked winner. The published standard entry tier is 200 Qualified MAU in one calendar month for $50/month, with higher usage tiers. The challenge deadline is November 30, 2026. Read the current platform rules again before publishing; the platform's qualified-usage numbers are authoritative.

- **October 20–24, proposed:** owner review, permission to upload, fresh-install Cloud Agent verification, live demo, then permission to resubmit.
- **October 25–31, proposed:** run the first 10–15 person pilot if the app is approved and accessible; fix observed usability failures.
- **November 1–30, proposed:** expand only after the pilot shows value. Track genuine qualified activity for a single calendar month. Do not add October and November users together.
- **If approval slips:** revise the calendar and eligibility estimate. Do not claim the threshold or reward has been achieved.

For a bundle-only app, a successful meaningful AI workflow using `llm.complete` can qualify; storage writes alone do not establish qualified usage. Developer/QA activity and synthetic test goals must not be counted as traction. Portfolio deduplication also matters if the same people use multiple submitted apps.
